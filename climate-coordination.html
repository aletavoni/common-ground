A QUICK FIELD GUIDE

How the game works

You play with five bots. All six players originally receive €40. Contributions in the first three rounds are fixed by the computer; you start making choices in round 4.

  • Equal history: everyone has already paid €6 and has €34 left.
  • Unequal history: three players have paid €0 and have €40 left; three have paid €12 and have €28 left.

Either way, the climate account already contains €36. During each of the seven remaining rounds, each player contributes €0, €2 or €4. Choices are simultaneous. After each round, everyone sees all individual contributions and totals.

If the final climate account is at least €120, everyone keeps their unspent money. If it is below €120, one common 50/50 draw decides whether everyone keeps their unspent money or everyone loses it. Contributions are never refunded. There is no private protection option.

Promises and actions

When pledges are enabled, you announce a total for rounds 4–10 before round 4, and a new total for rounds 8–10 before round 8. The intended climate account adds these promises to money already invested. A pledge does not move any money and is not binding. This version offers even-valued pledges that are feasible with the €0/€2/€4 choices.

Why call these Nash equilibria?

A Nash equilibrium is a set of plans where no player can improve their own expected payoff by changing their plan alone. The examples assume risk-neutral players who maximize their own expected money.

  • No further contributions: the target is out of reach for a single player. One player can add at most €28, while the group still needs €84. Spending zero is a best response.
  • Equal new contributions: everyone adds €14 and the account reaches €120. Equal-history players keep €20 for sure rather than the €17 expected from contributing zero. With unequal history, higher-budget players keep €26; lower-budget players keep €14, tying their €14 expected payoff from contributing zero.
  • Compensating contributions: higher-budget players add €20, lower-budget players add €8, and all keep €20 for sure. For higher-budget players, that ties the expected €20 from contributing zero; for lower-budget players, it exceeds the expected €14. A tie still qualifies as a best response.

There are other equilibria. Risk aversion, fairness concerns and expectations may change behavior. A lucky draw does not mean an unsuccessful contribution plan was better in expected-payoff terms.

The bot schedules

The equal-effort bots pay €2 in every active round. Compensating higher-budget bots pay €4 in rounds 4–6 and €2 in rounds 7–10; lower-budget bots pay €0 in rounds 4–6 and €2 in rounds 7–10. Gamble bots always pay €0. They keep these schedules regardless of your contributions or pledges.

These are selected pure-strategy Nash profiles from the start of the active game. If you deviate, the resulting play need not be an equilibrium. The schedules are not asserted to be subgame-perfect or optimal after every possible history. Pledges do not change the equilibrium schedules.

Research & teaching choices

Based on Tavoni et al. (2011), Inequality, communication, and the avoidance of disastrous climate change in a public goods game, PNAS 108, 11825–11829, especially Table 1 and the game description. The paper with supporting information describes the two pledge stages and common random draw.

The app preserves the group size, budgets, three fixed plus seven active rounds, contributions, target, feedback, pledge timing and 50% loss rule. It summarizes the fixed rounds instead of making you click through them, discloses the bot plans and lets you choose your position. A browser draw replaces the study’s physical lottery. Euros are fictional: no payments, carbon certificates or research questionnaires are involved. Bot schedules illustrate equilibria; they are not estimates of the study participants’ behavior.

The game runs on your device. Choices are kept in browser memory; refreshing resets the game. CSV downloads are made locally. No analytics or game server is used. A website host may keep ordinary access logs.
