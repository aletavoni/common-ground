THE FIELD GUIDE

A condition can change
everyone’s outcome.

You control one player, and bots control the others. Every player receives 10 fresh points in every round. Each either contributes all 10 to the group or keeps all 10.

The group’s contributions are multiplied by the group return (1.8 or 4.5), then divided equally among every player, including those who kept their points. Your payoff is your private 0 or 10 points plus your equal share of that public return.

Two institutions

Baseline: you can only choose always contribute or always keep. Since a contribution gives you back less than its 10-point cost, everyone keeping is the unique Nash equilibrium.

Common commitment: you can also say “I contribute only if at least k other players contribute.” The condition refers to others; you are not counted in your own threshold. “Always contribute” corresponds to k=0, and “always keep” to k=n, a threshold the other n−1 players can never meet.

The largest compatible outcome

Conditions can have more than one mutually compatible outcome. For example, if everyone contributes only if everyone else does, both universal cooperation and universal defection fit those conditions. The paper’s mechanism selects the most cooperative compatible outcome.

The trace starts with everyone as a candidate contributor, removes players whose conditions cannot be met, and repeats until no further removal is needed. This is a calculation, not a sequence of players observing and changing their choices. All actual choices are simultaneous, and no money moves until the final outcome is determined.

What the colors mean

Before resolution, tiles show commitments awaiting execution. At the final result, mint tiles contributed 10; coral tiles kept 10. In a trace, “still possible” means a candidate in the calculation, not a contribution already made. The final payoff panel always shows the settled result.

The bot equilibria

  • Everyone requires everyone: all players require the other n−1 players. Everyone cooperates under the maximal-outcome rule. If one player always keeps, the remaining commitments collapse, so deviating pays 10 instead of 18 (or 45 at the high return).
  • A cooperating coalition: m players each require m−1 others, and outsiders always keep. Members cooperate; outsiders benefit without contributing. A member withdrawing collapses the coalition. These profiles are equilibria when m is at least the smallest integer greater than or equal to n divided by the group return.
  • Everyone keeps: all choose always keep. One player cannot gain by contributing alone, because their private return is below 10. This is an equilibrium with either institution.

All selectable profiles are checked by comparing every player’s payoff with every legal unilateral deviation. A Nash equilibrium means nobody can earn more by changing their rule alone while others keep their rules. Some alternative thresholds tie. There are many other equilibria.

The bots repeat their selected thresholds. They do not learn from past rounds. Their executed contributions can nevertheless change when your current choice activates or unravels their commitments. Your deviations need not produce another equilibrium.

Counterfactual coach

After a round, the coach checks whether a lower threshold would both raise your own payoff and increase the number contributing, holding the bots’ thresholds fixed. It suggests the closest such threshold. The button opens an explanation; it never changes a submitted choice. Switching the coach off leaves all bot behavior and payoffs unchanged.

Research and teaching choices

Based on the supplied working paper by Marco Casari, Jesus Erubiel Ordaz-Cuevas and Alessandro Tavoni, “I Will If You Will” in Climate Mitigation: Conditional Cooperation in the Lab. Rules follow Sections 3–5 and Appendix A, particularly pp. 7–13 and 29–34.

The app includes all ten treatment combinations: baseline/common commitment for groups of 2, 3 and 6 with return 1.8; and groups of 15 with returns 1.8 or 4.5. It preserves the 10-point endowment, all-or-nothing contributions, simultaneous choices, payoff rule and largest-compatible-outcome mechanism. Thirty rounds is the original length; five rounds is a classroom shortcut.

Teaching additions include selected equilibrium bot profiles, disclosed bot thresholds, choice of coalition seat, the resolution trace, counterfactual comparisons after every eligible round and an optional coach toggle. The original experiment did not use these bots or this interface. The app illustrates incentives and the mechanism; it does not reproduce observed cooperation rates or estimate the effects of group size, returns or feedback on human behavior. No real cash conversion, minimum earnings guarantee, recruitment or questionnaire is included.

Thresholds throughout the app count other players, following the formal definition and experiment instructions. Appendix D sometimes discusses thresholds using a total-group convention; those vectors are not imported directly. All presets are verified under the convention shown here.

This game runs on your device, keeps play in browser memory and generates CSV exports locally. Refreshing resets it. There is no game backend or analytics; a web host may maintain ordinary access logs.
